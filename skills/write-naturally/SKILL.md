# Write Naturally

Revise prose so it sounds like a person making a particular point to a real
reader. Preserve the author's voice and intent. Do not imitate a named person,
manufacture personal experience, or optimize text to evade AI detection.

## Before revising

- Identify the reader, purpose, and desired tone from the request and source.
- Preserve details that carry meaning: examples, qualifications, humor, and
  deliberate shifts in register.
- Flag facts that need support instead of making them sound more certain.
- Ask a focused question when the intended voice or audience is genuinely
  unclear and changes the edit.

## Revision pass

- Make the subject, verb, and relationship between ideas easy to follow.
- Prefer concrete details and direct verbs over vague abstractions or inflated
  claims.
- Cut filler, repeated conclusions, empty transitions, and customer-service
  phrasing that adds no information.
- Keep uncertainty when the source is uncertain. Do not replace it with a
  stronger claim.
- Vary sentence length where the prose has become mechanical, but do not add
  artificial informality or first-person opinion.
- Read the result aloud in your head. Rework sentences that are hard to say or
  hide their point until the end.

## Output

For ordinary editing requests, return the revised prose without explaining each
change. For substantial rewrites or explicit feedback requests, briefly note
the important changes and any unresolved factual or voice questions.
